# Exercise Programming

## Triggers
- "build a training plan"
- "workout program"
- "how often should i train"
- "push pull legs"
- "progressive overload"
- "training split"
- "strength program"

## Purpose
Designs structured training programs: goal → split → progression scheme → deload policy, with volume and frequency matched to the trainee's actual experience and schedule.

## Safety

General fitness education, not medical or rehabilitation advice. Pain (especially sharp, joint, or radiating), known conditions, pregnancy, or recent injury go to a physician or physiotherapist first — say so and stop there.

## Voices
encouraging, concrete, technique-first

## Core Principle

Programs fail from too much too soon, not from too little. Adaptation comes from a repeatable stress applied progressively — the plan's job is to make "slightly harder than last time" automatic.

## Workflow

1. Establish the goal (strength, muscle gain, endurance, general health) and the real schedule (days per week, minutes per session).
2. Choose the split by frequency: beginners full-body 3x; intermediate push/pull/legs or upper/lower 3-4x; advanced PPL 5-6x.
3. Set working sets per muscle per week: 10-20 hard sets in range, starting at the low end for newcomers.
4. Pick a progression: add weight when all sets hit the top of the RPE range, else add reps — one variable at a time.
5. Anchor each session: warm-up ramp, compound lift first, accessories last, 1-2 reps in reserve on most sets.
6. Schedule deloads: every 4-6 weeks, cut volume ~40% or intensity ~10% before it is needed.
7. Define tracking: weight, reps, RPE per set — three columns, no journaling essays.

## Starter Template

| Day | Focus | Key lifts |
|-----|-------|-----------|
| A | Squat / press | Squat, bench or push press, row |
| B | Hinge / pull | Deadlift or Romanian, pull-up or pulldown, incline |
| C | Weak points | Split squat, OHP or dips, back work |

## Common Errors

- Adding exercises faster than load — junk volume collects while the main lift stalls.
- Testing strength instead of training it (maxing weekly).
- Ignoring sleep and protein, then blaming the program.
- Same intensity every session — autoregulate with RPE on bad days rather than skipping.

## Output Format

Deliver: the weekly split as a table, progression rule in one sentence, warm-up note, deload policy, and the two form cues most relevant to the chosen lifts. Reference the user's stated constraints explicitly.
