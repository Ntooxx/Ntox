# Sleep Optimization

## Triggers
- "improve my sleep"
- "can't fall asleep"
- "sleep hygiene"
- "fix my sleep schedule"
- "tired all the time"
- "wake up refreshed"
- "insomnia tips"

## Purpose
Builds a practical sleep-improvement protocol from the highest-leverage behavioral levers: consistent timing, light exposure, caffeine timing, temperature, and wind-down design — measured, not guessed.

## Safety

General sleep-hygiene education, not medical advice. Snoring with pauses, persistent insomnia beyond a few weeks, or daytime sleepiness that risks driving or operating machinery warrants a clinician visit (sleep apnea and other conditions are treatable) — flag that clearly.

## Voices
calm, low-stimulation, practical

## Core Principle

Sleep pressure and circadian timing are the two engines. Fix regularity first — same wake time seven days a week is the single change that drags bedtime, energy, and mood into line.

## Protocol

1. Lock the wake time (weekends included) and get outdoor light within 30-60 minutes of it — 10 minutes on bright days, longer when cloudy.
2. Cap caffeine: none within 8-10 hours of bedtime; note that afternoon "it doesn't affect me" coffee still cuts deep sleep.
3. Cool and dark: bedroom around 18-19C / 65-67F, blackout or eye mask, quiet or steady sound.
4. Build a 30-60 minute wind-down on repeat: dim lights, no email/work, same sequence nightly — the brain chains ritual to sleep.
5. Bed is for sleep (and intimacy): no work in bed; if awake over ~20 minutes, get up, dim light, return when sleepy.
6. Alcohol and late heavy meals are sedation, not sleep — move them earlier or cut them.
7. Track for two weeks: time to bed, time asleep, wake count, morning energy 1-5. Change one variable per week.

## Ceiling

Sleep need clusters near 7-9 hours. Chasing 10+ in bed fragments sleep — spend the extra hour awake getting more of the day instead.

## Output Format

Give the protocol as a numbered list sized to the person's actual habits, the one highest-leverage change first, a two-week tracking template, and the specific symptoms that mean see a doctor instead of self-tuning.
