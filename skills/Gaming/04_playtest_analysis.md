# Playtest Analysis

## Triggers
- "analyze playtest"
- "playtester feedback"
- "why do players quit"
- "fun is not working"
- "retention drop"
- "difficulty too hard"
- "turn feedback into changes"

## Purpose
Turns playtest sessions — notes, telemetry, and recordings — into a ranked, evidence-backed change list, separating what players felt from what players asked for.

## Core Principle

Players are reliable reporters of pain and unreliable prescribers of cures. Believe "this was frustrating" at 100%; discount "you should add a crafting system" to 20% until the underlying need is proven.

## Workflow

1. Gather three streams: observed behavior (where they went, how long), verbatim reaction (quotes!), and telemetry (funnel, deaths, drop-off points).
2. Segment before aggregating: first-timer vs experienced answers are different data; never average them into mush.
3. Build the experience timeline per tester: confusion moments, friction spikes, delight moments, quit moments.
4. Cluster findings by cause, not by symptom — five complaints may be one root problem.
5. Rank with a simple score: severity x frequency x confidence in the cause, minus fix cost.
6. Convert each finding into a change hypothesis: "because testers X, we will change Y, expecting metric Z."
7. Preserve the quotes — stakeholders act on stories backed by numbers, not scores alone.

## Finding Template

- Evidence: quote + timestamp + telemetry point
- Cause hypothesis: what we believe is actually wrong
- Change: the smallest intervention that tests the hypothesis
- Success metric: what moves if we are right

## Common Errors

- Designing by committee from the loudest tester.
- Fixing the reported symptom (add a hint!) instead of the cause (the level hides the door).
- Changing six things after one test — now nothing is attributable.
- Ignoring the testers who quit politely; their silence is data too.

## Output Format

Deliver: timeline of friction moments, the ranked findings table (severity, frequency, cause, change, metric), the 3 things NOT to change despite requests with reasons, and the single highest-leverage fix to test next session.
