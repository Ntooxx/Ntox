# Contract Review (First Pass)

## Triggers
- "review this contract"
- "read this agreement"
- "nda review"
- "is this contract fair"
- "msa review"
- "sign this contract"
- "contract red flags"

## Purpose
Performs a structured first-pass review of contracts (NDAs, MSAs, SOWs, employment offers) to surface risk, ambiguity, and missing protections — and to prepare precise questions for a human lawyer.

## Scope

This is a issue-spotting pass, not legal advice. Flag what a licensed attorney should examine and say so plainly; never tell the user a contract is safe to sign.

## Core Principle

Read every contract from the position of the party with less power, and ask one question per clause: if this goes wrong, who pays, who is trapped, and who can walk away?

## Workflow

1. Identify the parties, the exchange (money, services, data, IP), the term, and the renewal mechanism.
2. Read the money clauses first: payment terms, late fees, price changes, auto-renewal windows.
3. Read the exit clauses: termination for convenience, termination for cause, survival clauses, refund handling.
4. Read the risk clauses: liability caps (is there one? mutual?), indemnity (one-way is a red flag), insurance requirements.
5. Read the IP and data clauses: who owns work product, who licenses what back, confidentiality duration, data handling terms.
6. Read the dispute clauses: governing law, venue, arbitration, fee-shifting (loser pays?).
7. List ambiguities: undefined terms, "reasonable" without a number, missing acceptance criteria.

## Red Flag Table

| Flag | Why it matters |
|------|----------------|
| Uncapped liability | Your downside is unlimited |
| One-sided indemnity | You insure the other party |
| Auto-renewal with short opt-out | Locked in by calendar |
| IP assignment of "all works" | May capture unrelated work |
| Unilateral change terms | Contract can rewrite itself |
| No termination for convenience | Trapped for the full term |
| Vague deliverables | Dispute fuel |

## Output Format

Structure the review as: summary in three sentences, then a table of clauses rated OK / Concern / Red flag with the quoted language and a plain explanation, then 3-6 questions to negotiate or ask counsel — highest leverage first.
