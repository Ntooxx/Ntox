# Budget Analysis

## Triggers
- "analyze my budget"
- "where is my money going"
- "budget review"
- "spending analysis"
- "cut monthly costs"
- "budget variance"
- "am i overspending"

## Purpose
Analyzes a personal or team budget: categorizes spending, measures variance against plan, separates fixed from variable, and produces a ranked action list instead of a guilt chart.

## Voices
calm, concrete, non-judgmental, numbers first

## Core Principle

A budget is a policy for future money, not a verdict on past spending. The analysis earns its keep only if it changes at least one decision for next month.

## Workflow

1. Collect the actuals: 2-3 months of transactions or the last statement period.
2. Categorize every line; lump anything unrecognized into "review" rather than guessing.
3. Split each category into fixed (rent, salaries, subscriptions), variable (utilities, supplies), and discretionary (dining, upgrades).
4. Compare actual vs plan per category; compute variance in both currency and percent.
5. Rank categories by absolute waste potential, not percentage — 40% of a small line is noise, 5% of a big line is real money.
6. Check structural ratios: personal 50/30/20 (needs/wants/savings) as a health glance; teams use gross-margin and opex benchmarks for their stage.
7. Commit changes: for each cut, name the action (cancel, renegotiate, cap, keep) and the owner.

## Traps

- Averaging months with known one-offs hides the real run rate — annotate or exclude them.
- Percentage-only views over-weight tiny categories; always sort by currency.
- Squeezing variable categories forever while the fixed base grows is theater — challenge the big lines annually.
- Zero-based review once a year: every recurring charge must re-justify itself from zero.

## Output Format

Lead with total actual vs plan and the three largest actionable gaps. Then the variance table, then a ranked action list (savings per action, effort, when it takes effect), ending with what to keep spending on — budgets cut the wrong things when savings have no destination.
