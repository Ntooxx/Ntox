# Expense And Subscription Audit

## Triggers
- "audit my subscriptions"
- "find wasted spending"
- "cancel unused services"
- "expense audit"
- "find recurring charges"
- "vendor cost review"
- "what should i cancel"

## Purpose
Audits recurring expenses and recent charges to find waste, duplicates, and renegotiation targets — producing a keep/cancel/renegotiate decision list with the actual dollar impact of each.

## Voices
calm, specific, no shaming

## Core Principle

Recurring charges are decisions that stopped being made. The audit works by forcing every line to re-justify itself this month, not by finding one heroic cut.

## Workflow

1. Pull 90 days of transactions or the full subscription list; include annual renewals even if they are not monthly.
2. Group into recurring (same vendor, predictable cadence) and episodic.
3. For each recurring line, capture: amount, cadence, annualized cost, last meaningful use, and owner.
4. Classify: KEEP (clear value), RENEGOTIATE (used but over-market or duplicated), CANCEL (unused, superseded, or free tier exists).
5. Annualize everything — the psychological difference between 12x $9.99 and $120 is the point.
6. Check for overlaps: two tools doing one job, seats paid for past employees, plans above actual usage.
7. Sequence the actions: cancels with immediate effect first, renegotiation calls scripted with a target price and a walk-away, calendar reminders before every annual auto-renew.

## Decision Rules

- Unused for 60+ days with no scheduled use → cancel; re-subscribing takes five minutes.
- Downgrade beats cancel when usage is real but tier-crept (the "just in case" plan).
- Ask for the retention offer before canceling — but accept it only if it beats the alternative's price.
- Shared tools: split or reassign before canceling; personal cards funding team software is an accounting smell.

## Output Format

Table first: vendor, annualized cost, verdict, action, savings if acted. Then the top five actions ranked by dollars per minute of effort, a script line for renegotiations, and renewal-date reminders worth setting.
